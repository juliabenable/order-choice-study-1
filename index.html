The Choice Before the Card

Tony's Sep 10 idea, built: instead of landing on a card field, the creator lands on a question, how do you want to place the order, with two answers. Pay now and get reimbursed, or a free order backed by the Content Guarantee, marked Recommended. Tapping the free option opens the card as a bottom sheet, so the card arrives with its reason attached. This page holds the range we looked at first, the flow in the real 28 Litsea Delivery-step chrome, three alternates, the copy deck, and what pay-now would take to run.

The idea, in Tony's words

Sep 10 2026 · not yet decided

The trigger is a hypothesis, not a stall count yet: if more creators have a problem with the Content Guarantee the way it's framed today, this is one way to solve it. The card A/B (Aug 18) is still the running test. This design is built so it is ready the moment the numbers call for it.

TTony Staehelin · Sep 10 2026, 5:10am CEST · group DM (Tony, Katie, Julia) Proposed

It'll be interesting to see if more people have a problem with the content guarantee the way it's framed currently. If so, there's lots of ways we can solve it, but one idea is letting them choose: How do you want to place the order 1) Pay now, get reimbursed You pay for the order now, once you post you get reimburse the full order cost. Recommended 2) Free order, content guarantee The brand covers your order, you don't pay anything. You add a credit card to Benable as a guarantee, and we only charge the order if you never post the content.

I feel like that's less scary than seeing a credit card field. So they tap number two and then they put in the credit card and they know what it's for.

1What the idea is really doing. Two things at once. It gives the creator a choice where there was a demand, which takes the sting out of "why do you want my card". And it puts a priced alternative next to the card, so $0 today reads as the generous option instead of a catch. Pay-now is a real path, not a prop: creators who will not keep a card on file exist, and for them it is the honest answer. It just has to stay genuinely usable or the page becomes a dark pattern.
2The risk it introduces. The first thing the creator reads is a price. Until now money was invisible on this step. Tony's order (pay-now first) creates the relief read: "$52 now? Oh, or free." Alternate B keeps the good news first for the creator who never thought about money at all. That order is Tony's call, not settled here.
3What stays true from earlier decisions. The consent line sits next to the save button, no checkbox (Tony, Aug 20). The guarantee amount is the product price (Julia, Aug 20). No refund timeline is ever promised (Julia, Aug 20). The contact on the explainer stays kscalzo@benable.com (Katie). The production screenshot from Sep 9 has no Apple Pay, so none is added here.

Four are built at full fidelity in the sections below; the rest stay as sketches so they can be pulled forward if the test says so.

Built · alternate

What if the choice is an exit, not a menu?

Today's page plus one quiet line under the button: pay now instead. Smallest change; only helps the creator who already objects.

E

The receipt

What if we show the money instead of asking a question?

Order summary: two items, covered by 28 Litsea, you pay $0, guarantee: a card on file. Strong for "they know what it's for". Adds a page, offers no choice.

F

What happens next

What if the choice is shown as consequences over time?

Two rows: today, you post, after. Guarantee: $0, post, nothing happens. Pay-now: $52, post, $52 back. Explains the mechanics best; too much reading for a Delivery step.

G

Just the sentence

What if the framing alone fixes it?

No new UI. The top block states the trade: 28 Litsea covers your $52 order; a card on file is how we guarantee them a post. Costs nothing, ships with any of the above. The Sep 9 copy already moved this way.

H

Ask nothing

What if we ask nothing?

Proven creators skip the card: "Your track record covers this one." Already agreed as Katie's carve-out (Aug 18). Policy, not a page, but it is the strongest answer for repeat creators.

I

Three doors

What if we expose the exit?

Tony's two plus "I'd rather not add a card", routed to Katie. Honest, but it invites the exit. Aug 18 decided the escape hatch is Katie's email, never on screen.

J

Ask later

What if timing is the problem, not framing?

Add a card before your order ships, as a dated to-do; the order holds. Depends on whether the card really gates the order, which has been open since Sep 9.

tap either option · sheets are live · fields validate

Everything above the gray body is the production Delivery step as photographed on Sep 9: brand header, View Brief, the four-stage stepper. The body changes: the top block keeps the Sep 9 sentence and hands off to the question; two option cards replace the inline card form. Tap the free option and the card sheet slides up under the question, so the reason stays visible above it. Tap pay-now and the same sheet asks for a payment instead.

What to look at

1The question is the section title, in Tony's words. The Sep 9 top block stays the page title, so the screen has one title and one question, not two equal headings (the Sep 9 open item).
2The cards are the actions. No radio, no Continue button. Tony: "they tap number two and then they put in the credit card." The recommended card carries a purple edge and the Recommended tag; nothing else on the page uses color.
3The sheet explains itself in one line and links the full explainer (the rev-5 sheet, verbatim, with $52). $0 today, charged only if nothing is posted, final warning first. The consent line sits above Save and Continue, as decided Aug 20.
4Pay-now is real. Same fields, the button says Pay $52, and the line under the title promises the refund without a date. The done state shows what was chosen, with Change.
5Amount is dynamic. $52 is the creator's actual selection: Top Note Wild Citrus Body Oil at $24 and the flower exfoliant at $28, real 28litsea.com prices. It appears in the pay-now card, both sheets and the explainer, and nowhere else.
6Keyboard works. Tab through the cards, Enter opens the sheet, Esc or the scrim closes it. Use the rail below to jump to any state or fill a demo card.

Alternates at full fidelity

B swaps the order · C stays on one page · D is a footnote

Three ways to get most of the effect with a different shape. B is the same chooser with the good news first. C never leaves the current page: a segmented control sits above the fields and the button morphs between Save and Continue and Pay $52. D is the current page untouched, with one line under the lock text for the creator who would rather pay. C and D are live: the segments switch and the footnote opens the pay sheet.

BRecommended first reads calmer. The creator who has never thought about money sees $0 before they see $52. The cost is anchoring: with the price second, the free option no longer arrives as relief. If the A/B shows the problem is fear of the card rather than the trade, B is the better shape.
CSegmented is the engineering-cheapest version: the card form and its Stripe element stay where they are, the control only swaps copy and the CTA. But the card field is still the first thing on the screen, so it does not deliver Tony's "less scary than seeing a credit card field".
DFootnote changes nothing for the creator who is fine with the card, and gives the one who objects a way through without Katie. It is the version to ship if pay-now is meant as an escape hatch rather than a framing device.

06

What pay-now takes to run

the option has to be real, so here is the machinery behind it

The guarantee path is the one already specified (Stripe SetupIntent, Katie's final-warning ladder, the charge console). Pay-now is new. It is a purchase with a conditional refund, which touches Stripe, the ops console, the data model and the terms. None of it is hard; all of it needs an owner.

Stripe

  • Guarantee: SetupIntent, card saved off-session, nothing charged. Unchanged.
  • Pay-now: PaymentIntent for the product price, charged immediately. The card is not saved unless the creator opts in; that is the honest difference between the two options for a privacy-minded creator.
  • Refund: a full Stripe refund to the original payment method, triggered by the same event that releases a paid-campaign fee: Benable verifies the final links. Refund copy never names days.
  • 3DS may fire at payment time (it already can at save time); the bank sheet is already designed in the ghosting study.

Data and ops

  • creator_campaign.order_cover = guarantee | prepaid. Plus payment_intent_id, refund_status (none | due | refunded), and the amount at choice time (the amount ratchet already exists for the guarantee).
  • Katie's console gets a second population: prepaid creators are never charged and never warned about a charge; they get a refund action that arms when the post is verified.
  • The deadline ladder texts still go to prepaid creators; only the charge language drops out. Copy already exists for the control arm ("marked incomplete").
  • Brand-side line unchanged: creators have posted, we are following up with the rest.

Business calls before it can ship

  • Who keeps the money when a prepaid creator never posts? The brand shipped free product; the creator paid Benable. Remit to the brand, or keep it as the guarantee would have. Tony's call.
  • Terms. The Content Guarantee Terms cover the saved card. A purchase with a conditional refund needs its own two paragraphs (refund condition, no refund if no post, product kept). Julia's legal pass.
  • Test design. Is the chooser a third arm next to card and no-card, or the replacement for the card arm once stalls cross the Aug 18 line (about 25% stalls with under 10 points of post-rate lift)?

What to measure

  • Chooser view → option tapped → sheet completed, per option. The number that matters is completion of the step versus today's inline card form.
  • Share choosing pay-now. If it rounds to zero, the option is doing its job as framing; if it is material, ops needs the refund flow on day one.
  • Stall at the sheet (opened, never completed) versus stall at the page (never tapped). They need different fixes: copy versus fear of the card.
  • Post rate by cover type, once a cohort closes. Prepaid creators have real money on the line, which is Stack Influence's whole model.
